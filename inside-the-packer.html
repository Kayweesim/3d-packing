The vocabulary

What we pack, and which way is which

The unit

A carton is one product box (W×H×D). Cartons group into a pallet (a shipment unit with an ID). Pallets load into a container.

Axis convention

x = width (235 cm) · y = height · z = depth. Crucially z = 0 is the back wall and z = d is the door — the packer fills back-to-front.

The core idea

The packer thinks in free spaces

The container is modelled as a list of empty rectangular cuboids. It starts as one free space equal to the full interior. Every time a carton lands, the space it used is carved into smaller free spaces — and the list grows.

The used space splits into up to three sub-spaces.

For each carton the engine scans every (free space × orientation) pair, scores the legal ones, and commits the best — then re-splits. Simple primitives, repeated thousands of times.

One carton at a time

The placement loop

Try every option

For each free space, test all axis orientations the carton allows (1 if rotation is off, up to 6 otherwise).

Drop it in

Place at the space's back-left corner (x, z); compute y by gravity-settling onto whatever sits below the footprint.

Reject the impossible

Throw out candidates that float, exceed the space's ceiling, or that a loader can't reach.

Keep the best

Score the survivors, commit the lowest score, then split its host space. Strict < means ties keep the first-seen option.

Gravity-settling never floats a box: y is always the highest surface directly beneath its footprint, or the floor.

After every placement

The guillotine split

The placed carton carves its host space into three non-overlapping sub-spaces. They tile the leftover volume exactly — no overlaps, no gaps in the bookkeeping.

Front — toward doorRight — beside the box
Above — stack on top

Two cut orders

"front" (default) — Front keeps the full height & width, giving later cartons a clear front lane.

"above" — Above keeps the full width & depth: one contiguous stacking slab so stacks flush to the back. Best for stacking-heavy loads.

How the best spot wins

Scoring: depth-first

Every legal candidate gets a tuple. Lower wins. The packer compares them like words in a dictionary — first by depth, then height, then sideways.

score = ( pz, py, px )
pz  deepest first — fill each z-slice before advancing to the door
py  then lowest — gravity, build the floor before stacking
px  then leftmost — tidy left-to-right within the slice

Orientation is not in the score

Because the tuple ignores the box's dimensions, two orientations that settle at the same depth & height tie — and the strict < keeps whichever was tried first: the carton's original orientation. (This is exactly the gap the algo2 orientation strategies later exploit.)

Why candidates get rejected

Three physical constraints

Full support

At least 50% of a carton's base must rest on coplanar tops below it. Sub-100% lets rigid boxes bridge small gaps for denser packs.

Reachability

No placing behind a wall taller than a 50 cm arm's reach, and no deep pockets unless the free lane is a walkable ≥ 50 cm aisle.

Stacking flag

If a carton is marked stacking = false, the support check refuses anything resting on top of it — fragile items stay clear.

These run before scoring — an unreachable or floating candidate never even competes.

Between pallets

Coalescing the leftovers

A finished pallet leaves its free space fragmented at its own grid pitch — e.g. one thin "Above" shelf per column. Before the next pallet packs, any two free spaces that share a full face are fused back into one.

→

Coalesced

Now the next pallet packs at its pitch instead of inheriting the previous one's grid — closing the seams that appear between differently-sized pallets. Fusing full-face neighbours is always geometrically safe.

Load order for the animation

Topological sort

Packing decides positions; a second pass decides the order the boxes appear in. It runs Kahn's BFS over the support graph so a box never lands before the one beneath it.

The hard rule

An edge j → i exists wherever carton j's top directly supports i's bottom. Supporters always animate first — physically honest.

The tie-break

(seq_rank, centre_z, y, x) → pallet order, then back-to-door, then bottom-up, then left-right. seq_rank decouples a pallet's colour from its load order, so algo2 can reorder pallets while colours stay put.

Result: every carton of pallet N animates before any carton of pallet N+1, and within a pallet they fill back-to-front, then bottom-up.

Stable vs. secured loads

Lashing & the flat re-pack

A half-empty last container packed depth-first becomes a tall back wall — a topple risk. With lashing off, that container is re-packed into a height-capped copy so the load sits low and forward.

depth-first → topple
→
flat → stable

How the cap is found

Start at a volume-based height estimate, then raise the cap one carton-layer at a time until every box fits. Kept only if it loses nothing — otherwise the dense version stands.

Lashing = on

The load is strapped, so tall depth-first stacking is fine — the flat re-pack is skipped entirely.

Choosing the containers

The optimizer wraps the packer

The packer fills a given set of containers. The optimizer decides which set — searching cheapest-first until everything fits.

Generate

All (n20, n40) combos under a cost cap, sorted by (cost, count, n20) — cheapest, then fewest boxes.

Pre-check

Skip any combo whose total volume already can't hold the cartons — no point packing it.

Stop early

Run the packer on each; return the first that packs everything, else the best partial attempt.

20ft = 1.0, 40ft = 1.5. The result feeds the 3D scene and the Excel load plan directly.

Turning the whole group

Orientation strategies

Because the greedy engine keeps the original orientation on ties, a group can tile the width poorly. algo2 adds strategies that rotate the whole group uniformly — and measures whether it packs tighter.

Width-fit

Rotate so the most columns fit across the 235 cm width — kills the wasted lane on the right.

Stand-tall

Largest dimension vertical → smaller footprint → more cartons per floor slice.
